# HerRays Product Intelligence & Innovation Lab

**Strategy and execution playbook**  
**Version:** 1.0 · **Date:** 22 November 2026 · **Status:** Proposed, not executed  
**Owner:** HerRays Head of Product

## Executive mandate

Create a distinctive, clinically responsible, enjoyable lifestyle and behavior-change experience for women in midlife, integrated where appropriate with HerRays' physician-led care and longitudinal health intelligence. Do not build a collage of competitor features. Investigate user problems, identify mechanisms, grade evidence, invent alternative experiences, validate with real people, and only then select an MVP.

**Operating architecture:** eight coordinated workstreams; five core benchmark apps and 15–20 total comparison products; 13 original specialists plus clinical/privacy reviewers; 30–50 deep capability records; six independent concept studios; five quality gates; a proposed 12-week discovery/design cycle. Timelines are planning estimates, not completed work. Long-term behavior change and health outcomes require follow-up beyond this cycle.

**Research principle:** distinguish (1) directly observed product behavior, (2) public user reports, (3) scientific evidence, (4) market estimates, (5) expert interpretation, (6) untested invention. Apple editorial recognition is not proof of clinical efficacy or retention. Reverify any claimed App of the Day dates and geographies before using them as evidence.

## 1. Outcomes and success measures

- Deliver a source-linked evidence base, not merely presentations.
- Understand why users initiate, maintain, abandon and restart lifestyle behaviors.
- Separate engagement, adherence, habit automaticity and health outcomes.
- Identify mechanisms worth adopting, adapting, inventing around, rejecting or deferring.
- Generate six meaningfully different product visions, with independent initial concept work.
- Test desirability, comprehension, usability, trust, accessibility and plausible repeat value.
- Produce editable wireframes, end-to-end journeys, a build-versus-buy plan, an MVP specification, and a subsequent longitudinal validation plan.
- Maintain clinical safety, privacy, user autonomy, accessibility and non-patronizing HerRays brand principles as non-negotiable constraints.

## 3. Benchmark portfolio and documented examples

### Core five (mandatory deep dives)

| App | Primary learning question | Capabilities to inspect | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Routinery | How does intention become action? | Guided timers, routine sequences, reminders, completion | Rigidity and pressure are hypotheses to test |
| Fabulous | How do abstract goals become guided behavior programs? | Onboarding, coaching, narratives, habit plans, paywalls | Engagement does not establish clinical efficacy |
| (Not Boring) Habits | How does functional tracking become delightful? | 3D feedback, motion, rewards, visual identity | Novelty and animation costs need testing |
| Habitify | When is tracking useful rather than work? | Analytics, progress, reminders, integrations | Charts may not translate into decisions |
| Griply | How do daily actions connect to life goals? | Goals, tasks, habits, progress hierarchy | Planning overhead may reduce utility |

These are research starting points, not a claim that all five have verified App of the Day recognition in the requested 2023–2026 period. Record the country/date/source for any editorial recognition.

### Expanded portfolio

Target 15–20 apps, including relevant direct habit products (e.g. Streaks, Productive), adjacent health experiences (e.g. Finch, Gentler Streak, Headspace, Oura, selected women's health apps), and exceptional consumer references (e.g. Duolingo, Apple Fitness). Add only if the app answers a distinct question. Include negative and discontinued examples where useful. Each entry must declare whether it is a direct competitor, behavior reference, visual reference, health-data reference or commercial reference.

### Strong documented examples: how they are used

1. Register each app and every verifiable example in the evidence repository, including source URL, captured date, app version, country and whether it is directly observed or secondary.
2. Recreate the relevant journey using actual app access where possible; record screenshots and interaction sequences, respecting copyright and access restrictions.
3. Ask each specialist to analyze the same example independently.
4. Translate the visible feature into user job, underlying mechanism, possible benefit, downside, evidence, alternatives and validation question.
5. Cross-check against published research and primary user needs.
6. Preserve original examples as annotated reference patterns for designers, **not mandatory features**.
7. Link every adopted/adapted/rejected design decision to its original example and other supporting or contradictory evidence.

Use existing HerRays OS/Notion as source of truth where available, with structured exportable tables and linked artifacts. Avoid buying multiple repository tools at the outset.

| Capability Library | User job, feature, mechanism, evidence, alternatives, risks, tests | CAP-001 |

| Pattern Library | Good/bad pattern, examples, trade-offs, accessibility | PAT-001 |
| Opportunity Atlas | Problem, evidence, gap, HMW, alternatives, feasibility | OPP-001 |
| Experiment Registry | Hypothesis, method, success criteria, sample, results, decision | EXP-001 |
| Decision Log | Option, adopt/adapt/invent/reject/defer, rationale, reviewer, date | DEC-001 |

**Evidence grades:** A = strong relevant science or replicated direct validation; B = credible but limited scientific/direct user evidence; C = repeated qualitative observations or competitor patterns; D = expert judgment, analogy or invention. Grade separately for efficacy, relevance and confidence where needed. Do not interpret a high grade on usability as a high grade on health impact.

**Initial evidence targets:** 15–20 apps; 30–50 high-relevance publications (prioritize systematic reviews, randomized studies and implementation evidence); approximately 20–30 discovery interviews with purposive sampling; 7–14-day diary research if feasible; several hundred sampled public reviews stratified by rating, recency and version; market estimates clearly distinguished from verified metrics. These are planning targets, not completed data.

**Source handling:** Record URLs, access dates, evidence quotes/excerpts within legal limits, methods, confidence, contradictions, provenance and what remains unknown. Never fabricate interview participants, actual retention, CAC, LTV, clinical outcomes or internal competitor data.

## 5. Standardized competitive intelligence workflow

### Phase 0: research operations (days 1–3)

Approve scope, select apps, assign owners, set naming conventions, create databases, templates, quality checklist, access register, assumptions register, decision log and research dashboard. Establish consent and privacy rules before collecting primary research.

### Phase 1: market intelligence (week 1–2)

Collect App Store listing and metadata, price/subscription terms, app updates, verified editorial features, positioning, public company statements, user reviews, available download/revenue estimates and visible features. Sample reviews by rating/date/version, code themes with independent agreement checks on a subset. Do not infer retention from reviews or downloads. Deliver app dossiers, market positioning, monetization comparison and gaps.

### Phase 2: controlled app teardown (weeks 2–3)

Use consistent devices, app versions, regions, access levels and research scenarios. Initial provisional scenarios: motivated beginner, repeated habit abandoner, unpredictable schedule, low-energy day, tracking-averse user, experienced optimizer. Replace/refine with observed user segments after interviews.

**Ten mandatory journeys:** (1) discovery, (2) first launch/onboarding, (3) personalization/goal selection, (4) first meaningful action, (5) normal daily interaction, (6) reminders/re-entry, (7) progress/reward/reflection, (8) missed day/recovery, (9) change of goal/schedule/capacity, (10) paywall/cancellation/return.

Record taps, time, decisions, copy, screen transitions, friction, emotion hypotheses, error states, accessibility and platform behavior. Repeat use over 7–14 days where possible; this only reveals short-term experience, not long-term retention. Independently commission behavioral, UX, visual/motion, growth/content, and iOS/accessibility audits for each core app, then reconcile disagreements.

### Phase 3: capability mechanism extraction (weeks 3–4)

Extract **30–50 deep records** using five layers: observed feature → user job → mechanism → evidence/limitations → alternative solutions. Each record includes app/source reference, user context, benefits, failure modes, ethics, HerRays relevance, three alternatives plus one unconventional alternative, technical implications, test hypothesis and decision status.

**Worked example: streaks.** Feature: consecutive-day counter. Job: make effort visible and encourage continuation. Hypothesized mechanisms: reinforcement, feedback, commitment. Risks: shame and all-or-nothing behavior after a missed day. Alternatives: flexible weekly consistency; cumulative effort/milestones; narrative progress; celebrate returning after disruption. Required test: do alternatives improve meaningful repeated action and perceived autonomy, not merely app opens?

**90-minute extraction workshop per important mechanism:** 0–15 observation/user job (UX research); 15–30 mechanism/science (behavioral scientist); 30–45 friction/edge cases (UX/QA); 45–60 independent alternatives (all); 60–75 novel/technical options (creative/iOS); 75–90 document tests and uncertainties (product). Do not finalize features in this workshop.

### Phase 4: capability review (weeks 4–5)

First apply mandatory clinical, privacy, ethics, accessibility and unsupported-claim gates. Then assess user value (25%), behavioral evidence (20%), experience quality (15%), differentiation (15%), long-term usefulness (15%) and feasibility (10%). These weights are a proposed prioritization heuristic, not a validated scientific instrument. Red-team every high-priority item. Record **Adopt / Adapt / Invent / Reject / Defer** and rationale, confidence, dependencies, smallest test and owner. Do not turn this into a feature shopping list.

## 6. Parallel scientific, user and technical research

**Behavioral science:** Use COM-B, BCTTv1, Self-Determination Theory, habit formation, implementation intentions and relevant intervention studies. Separate engagement, adherence, automaticity and clinical outcomes. Extract populations, interventions, comparators, duration, effect sizes when available, uncertainty and transferability to midlife women. Assess risks of guilt, compulsion, burden and excessive tracking.

**User discovery:** Recruit a varied set of women in the target audience, including differences in health goals, schedules, tech comfort, prior success/failure and tracking tolerance. Ask about concrete past behavior and workarounds, not just imagined preferences. Use neutral interviews, observation and optional diary study. Produce JTBD, journey maps, unmet needs, evidence and contradictions. Obtain consent and minimize sensitive data.

**Technology/economics:** Evaluate native iOS, HealthKit, notifications, widgets, on-device personalization, backend sync, identity, privacy/security, clinical integration boundaries, accessibility, App Store policy, analytics, subscriptions and operating costs. For each capability choose build, buy, integrate or defer; document complexity, vendor dependency, maintenance, data ownership, regulatory implications and differentiation.

## 7. Opportunity synthesis: never jump directly from evidence to concepts

After evidence review, identify root user problems, contradictions, unmet needs, underserved contexts and jobs. Produce **at least 12 opportunity statements**, then group into **4–6 distinct territories**. Each opportunity includes problem/context, evidence/confidence, competitor gap, desired outcome, relevant mechanism, constraints, How Might We statement and validation path. Include opportunities that need clinical integration and ones that do not. Gate approval before concept studios begin.

**Example:** Rather than 'build a better progress dashboard,' explore 'how might we reassure users that their efforts matter without forcing them to become data analysts?'

## 8. Six independent concept studios

All studios receive the same versioned evidence package, opportunity atlas, capability decisions, real user findings, brand principles, clinical/privacy/accessibility constraints and success measures. They must **not** see each other's initial designs before the first review.

| Studio | Mandate |
|---|---|
| A — Behavior First | Make change achievable for people who repeatedly abandon habits |
| B — Joy First | Create emotional delight and intrinsic return value |
| C — Invisible App | Deliver meaningful value with minimal daily screen interaction |
| D — Health Intelligence | Connect daily choices to personally relevant health context |
| E — Life Companion | Adapt to changing energy, schedules and priorities |
| F — Category Breaker | Challenge every convention of habit apps |

Each studio: interpret evidence; independently generate at least 15 divergent ideas; critique; select three contrasting concepts; create proposition, promise, first-use/ordinary-day/missed-day/long-term journeys, behavioral logic, novel elements, risks and storyboards; then prototype. Six studios × three developed concepts = **18 initial concept dossiers**. Select about six directions for deeper testing; do not jump immediately to polished screens.

## 9. Validation and selection

Test desirability, comprehension, first-action completion, perceived effort, emotional appeal, trust, differentiation, accessibility, return intention and willingness to pay. Use realistic tasks, neutral questions, observed behavior, counterbalanced concept order and representative users. Distinguish stated preference, usability, observed repeat behavior and long-term change. The latter require longer pilots. Redesign/retest weak areas and record uncertainty.

**Final selection weights (proposed):** behavior-change potential 25%; desirability/delight 20%; ease of daily use 20%; differentiation 15%; long-term value 10%; feasibility 10%. Clinical safety, privacy and accessibility are mandatory gates, not compensable weighted scores. Do not combine incompatible interaction philosophies simply to retain popular features.

**Wireframe minimum for finalists:** welcome, discovery, first action, daily home, guided action, progress/reflection, missed day/recovery, personal insight; add personalization, reminders, errors, privacy controls, settings, paywall and accessibility states as needed. Provide editable files, annotated interactions and source-to-decision traceability.

## 10. Build vs buy and purchasing gates

**Build proprietary:** differentiated habit/action engine, adaptive journeys, coaching logic, progress semantics, personalized recommendations within validated scope, HerRays-specific health intelligence interfaces, clinician workflows, brand/interaction language and core IP.

**Buy/integrate selectively:** research reference access, collaborative design tooling, production analytics, crash reporting, subscription infrastructure if relevant, standard infrastructure and security services. Do not recreate a mature billing or analytics platform without a compelling reason.

| Stage | Recommended tools | Purchase rule |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–14 | Existing HerRays OS/Notion, spreadsheets, App Store, free Figma | No new paid research stack; buy paid benchmark app access only when required |
| Weeks 2–3 | Mobbin / Page Flows, Appfigures | Trial/one month only after confirming coverage or missing intelligence; avoid overlapping subscriptions |
| Weeks 3–5 | AppFollow, Dovetail | Defer unless review/interview volume justifies it |
| Weeks 6–9 | Figma paid tier | Upgrade only when collaboration/prototyping exceeds free tier |
| Weeks 9–12 | Maze or similar | Only if it improves an actual test plan; prioritize participant recruitment/incentives |
| MVP development | Amplitude **or** Mixpanel, Crashlytics, RevenueCat if applicable | Choose at implementation time based on data minimization, consent and billing model |

Appfigures/Sensor Tower estimates are not actual competitor analytics. Mobbin and Page Flows may not cover niche apps or current flows; verify coverage before paying. RevenueCat is conditional on in-app subscription architecture and App Store rules. Avoid buying multiple analytics platforms. Preserve budget for real user testing, specialist review and clinical/regulatory work.

## 11. Five quality gates

1. **Research foundation:** benchmark coverage, source-linked evidence, unknowns and access limitations documented.
2. **Opportunity readiness:** usable primary research, reviewed behavioral mechanisms, opportunities distinct from feature requests.
3. **Concept readiness:** independent studio outputs, complete journeys, invention clearly distinguished from evidence.
4. **Validation readiness:** testable prototypes, preregistered tasks/criteria, safety/privacy/accessibility review.
5. **Build readiness:** critical usability risks addressed, approved MVP/architecture, remaining clinical/behavioral claims have a validation plan.

At each gate, the program director issues pass / conditional pass / fail, owner, missing evidence, remediation and decision date. Polished unsupported reports do not pass.

## 15. Immediate launch order

1. Run Prompt 00 and create the evidence infrastructure and app access register.
2. Launch Prompts 01, 02 and 03 in parallel; prepare Prompt 04 recruitment immediately and begin Prompt 05 when requirements are available.
3. Acquire paid benchmark app access only when necessary for actual flows; check Mobbin coverage before subscribing.

**Final decision rule:** build the product that delivers meaningful user value with the least unnecessary effort, not the one with the most features, highest engagement count, or prettiest prototype. HerRays' differentiation must come from the combined quality of everyday experience, appropriate personalization, credible health intelligence, and trustworthy care boundaries.
